# Audit: did the `feat/laya-decision-speed` branch nerf or clog the system? (2026-11-06)

Short answer: no clogging and a large speed gain, but the branch did introduce three regressions. All three are
fixed and flown here. Laya itself is not in the system: it was evaluated and rejected, so nothing of it runs at
mission time.

Evidence: recorded episodes in `logs/episodes/`, model-only replays of recorded decisions
(`scripts/autonomy/replay_decision_latency.py`), and headless flights on AC power with the stock `dcm-fly`
(logs in `output/audit-20261106/`).

## Prompt size ("clogged with information?")

| | Before (2026-10-24) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt at the first decision | 7,994 tokens | 8,399 tokens (+5 %) |
| Tool catalogue | 26.8-28.2k chars, changed during the mission | 28.5k chars, fixed |
| Tokens the model actually reads per decision | all ~8-9.6k (cache missed) | median 145 new tokens |

## Where mission time goes (13 flights, 2026-11-06 02:00-03:30)

- Wall time 3,423 s: model decisions 223 s (**7 %**), camera questions ~704 s (21 %, mostly concurrent with
  survey flight), the rest is flying.
- Inside a decision (median 2.2 s): generating the tool call 1.8 s, prefill 0.26 s.

Further decision-speed work can move a mission by a few percent at most; the work below is about robustness.

## Regression 1 (fixed): camera questions shared the mission's server slot

With one slot, llama-server copied the displaced prompt into its RAM cache on every switch between the mission
conversation and an image: +~110 MiB per camera question (1.9 -> 4.2 GiB after 15), and after a 53-88 frame
survey the mission conversation was evicted (the next decision took 10-11 s, 3 of 3 cases).

Fix (`python/dcm/llama_runtime.py`): the mission and the camera each pin a slot
(`--parallel 2 --kv-unified --cache-ram 0`). Flown: llama-server memory stayed at 2.5-3.7 GiB across 33
flights; the decision right after a full survey took 1.5-1.9 s with the cache intact. Cost: +54 MiB of GPU
memory, no change in speed.

## Regression 2 (fixed): orbit missions gained a step, and it could fail the mission

After `search_for_object` found the object, the model stopped calling `orbit` directly:

| Next call after a successful search | orbit | approach_target | localize_target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-24 (15 cases) | 9 | 4 | 1 |
| 2026-11-06 before the fix (16 cases) | 1 | 6 | 7 |

The choice is a near-tie that flips on tiny numerical differences (replaying the same recorded request on
today's server gives `approach_target` whether the catalogue is old or new, cached or re-read). It mattered:
`approach_target` from 10 m altitude cannot keep a LiDAR lock on a low house, so "find the nearest house and
orbit it" failed where a direct orbit works.

Fix (`python/dcm/skills.py`): the search result now says what it is: "Seen by the camera, not measured yet:
orbit, approach_target and overfly_target each measure it and fly to their own standoff in the one call."
Replay: 9 of 9 Oct 24 orbit cases and 8 of 9 Nov 6 orbit cases go straight to `orbit`, while the "go close,
then orbit" mission still approaches first (7 of 7). Flown: house orbit completed in 5 decisions (was 6-8).
Four tool-description rewordings were tried first and did nothing; text in the result, where the next call is
chosen, is what moved it.

## Regression 3 (fixed): the 20 Hz position-rate change

Requesting LOCAL_POSITION_NED at 20 Hz (commit c8ec8ec) was meant to cure stale-state aborts. Those were
caused by the laptop being on battery. Same seven AirSim missions, fresh sim each time:

| Position rate | Completed | LiDAR geometry errors ("re-measured surface is 16 m away", "lost the target", "obstacle inside emergency envelope") |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Hz | 2 of 7 | in 3 missions |
| 5 Hz (original) | 5 of 6 finished | none |

One run each, in a noisy sim, with no mechanism found by reading the code, so this is evidence, not proof. The
change is reverted: the original rate has the long flight history and 20 Hz showed no benefit on AC.

## Also fixed in this pass

- **False "incomplete"**: the success check flagged a redundant `arm` that was refused because the drone was
  already armed. Calls refused because their goal already holds (`REASON_CODE_ALREADY_*`) no longer count.
- **Empty search reported as a count**: when "how many cars" was flown as a turn in place, the model reported 0
  cars. The empty-search result now says it "has not counted anything; counting over an area is survey_count".
  Replay: 2 of 2 such cases go on to the survey; 0 of 12 find-and-orbit cases were pushed into a survey.

## Tested and rejected

- **Telling the model to omit default parameters** (research idea 1): output tokens fell 2 %, and 10 of 170
  recorded decisions changed, some for the worse (a survey became "move forward 10 m"). A one-sentence prompt
  edit flips ~6 % of this model's decisions; prompt edits need the replay check.
- **A more permissive success-check wording**: cleared the false alarms but let a truly failed survey be
  reported as completed. The strict wording stays.
- **Camera accuracy at the 256-token CPU cap**: 69 of 72 on the labelled frames (68 of 72 at full size), so the
  cap costs nothing (`vision-bench/resident_cpu256_results.json`).

## Still open

- **Surveys do not route around tall objects.** At 20 m in the AirSim city the route often meets one; the
  safety layer stops it (correctly) and the retry is refused as too far from home.
- **Choices flip on tiny state differences.** "altitude 3.0 m" vs "2.9 m" in the state text changed
  `survey_count` into `search_for_object`. The result-text fixes catch the two cases seen; the fragility itself
  is the 4B model's.
- **Conservative false "incomplete"**: a recovery attempt that failed and was then made good by another tool
  still downgrades the outcome (the message explains what happened).
- "Take a look around" checks one view; hedged camera answers count as found (the LiDAR gate blocks acting on
  them).

## Research: other UAV agent projects

Full survey: [UAV-AGENT-ARCHITECTURES.md](UAV-AGENT-ARCHITECTURES.md). No surveyed project gets good results
from a zero-shot 3-4B model as a multi-step UAV agent without fine-tuning, and Icarus already has what the
stronger designs converge on (composite skills, static catalogue, executor guardrails, receipt check).

| Idea (source) | Verdict here |
|---|---|
| Executor-owned defaults (TypeFly, Octopus v2) | Tested as a prompt instruction: rejected (see above). |
| Start executing while the next call is generated (TypeFly) | ~2 % of mission time, runs steps without seeing the previous result. No. |
| Failure results that state what they are and what comes next (SPINE, BTGenBot-2) | Adopted in spirit: the two result-text fixes above. The remaining candidates are orbit and survey aborts. |
| Compact call syntax / DSL (MiniSpec) | Leaves the model's trained tool format; no zero-shot small model shown doing it. No. |
| Tool retrieval, functional tokens, plan caching, single-shot plans | Save costs we no longer pay; tool retrieval would break the cache. No. |
| LoRA fine-tune on own traces (SPINE, TinyAgent, DroneCATS) | The only lever shown to close the small-model gap, and the real answer to the choice fragility. Later. |
